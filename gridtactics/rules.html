A game of small armies and tight squares

Grid Tactics

Two commanders, one 8 × 10 battlefield. Lead Bruisers, Spearmen and Archers to take the enemy base or hold the field, before your opponent does it to you.

2 players About 30 minutes Learn in 10 minutes Draft rules 0.4, still being tested
The goal

Three ways to win

The first player to do any one of these wins immediately.

Capture

Take the enemy base

Move any one of your units onto your opponent's base square. You win the moment it arrives.

Points

Score 12 victory points

Hold the two control points. Each one you are standing on at the start of your turn scores 1 point.

Wipe out

Destroy their army

Your opponent has no units left on the board and nothing left in their reserve to bring in.

The battlefield

Know the board

Bases. Blue's base is e1 at the bottom; Orange's is d10 at the top. New units arrive next to your base. Guard yours, because one enemy unit stepping onto it ends the game.

Control points at a5 and h6. Stand on them to score victory points.

High ground at e6 and f6. Spearmen and Archers standing here reach 1 square further.

Walls at b7 c7 and f4 g4. Nothing can stand on or move through a wall. Attacks pass over them.

Starting armies. Each side begins with one Bruiser (B), one Spearman (S) and one Archer (A) in the row in front of its base.

Who beats whom

  • Bruisers hunt Archers. They close the distance fast, charge for 3, and an adjacent Archer can't hit back.
  • Spearmen stop Bruisers. A charging Bruiser takes a first strike before it lands its blow.
  • Archers break Spearmen. They shoot from out of reach, and a Spearman holding its ground takes 3.

Both sides get the same reserve, so the skill is in timing: watch what your opponent fields and bring in the answer when it counts.

Before you start

Setup

  1. Choose sides

    Decide at random who plays Blue. Blue sits at the bottom (row 1) and takes the first turn. Orange sits at the top.

  2. Place the starting armies

    Blue: Bruiser on d2, Spearman on e2, Archer on f2. Orange: Bruiser on c9, Spearman on d9, Archer on e9.

  3. Set out your reserve

    Beside the board, each player keeps 2 Bruisers, 2 Spearmen and 2 Archers. These are the only reinforcements you get all game.

  4. Set the score

    Blue starts on 0 victory points and Orange on 2. Orange moves second, and the head start makes up for it.

Playing the game

On your turn

Players take turns, starting with Blue. A round is one turn each. Every turn follows the same two steps, in order.

  1. Score

    Gain 1 victory point for each control point one of your units is standing on. If you reach 12, you win.

  2. Take up to 5 actions

    Each action is either recruiting one unit or activating one of your units to move and/or attack. You may recruit once per turn, as your first action. Each unit can be activated only once per turn. Unused actions are lost.

The rules in detail

Moving, fighting, recruiting

Moving

  • Move up, down, left or right, one square at a time, up to the unit's Move value. No diagonal steps.
  • You can't enter or pass through walls or any other unit, including your own.
  • Enemies hold you up. When a unit steps onto a square next to an enemy, it must stop there. A unit that starts next to an enemy may still walk away.

Attacking

  • Count range like movement, squares up, down, left and right, but walls and units don't block it.
  • Bruisers and Spearmen attack at range 1 (next door). Archers attack at range 2 to 4.
  • On high ground, Spearmen and Archers add 1 to their maximum range. Bruisers don't.
  • Hitting back. If the target survives and the attacker is within the target's range, the target strikes back once, right away.

Special abilities

  • Charge counts squares moved in this action only. It doesn't apply when a Bruiser hits back.
  • First strike happens when the enemy finishes its move. If it kills the unit, that unit's attack is lost. Every enemy Spearman next to the stopping square strikes. Placing a new recruit doesn't trigger it.
  • Steady aim also applies when an Archer hits back. A unit recruited this turn hasn't moved yet, so it counts as standing still.

Recruiting

  • Take any unit from your reserve and place it on an empty square next to your base (up, down, left or right of it). Recruiting is free; your reserve is the only limit.
  • You can have at most 6 units on the board.
  • A new unit can be activated later in the same turn.
  • If every square around your base is filled, you can't recruit.
An example turn

Breaking a point

An Orange Spearman (5 health) has held the control point on h6 for several turns, scoring every time. Blue has an Archer on f5 and a Bruiser on h3.

  1. The Archer shoots first. h6 is 3 squares away (2 across, 1 up), within range 2–4. The Spearman didn't move last turn, so steady aim deals 3. Spearman: 5 → 2. The Spearman's range is 1, so it can't hit back.
  2. The Bruiser charges. It moves h4, h5. Being next to the Spearman, it must stop there. That's 2 squares, so charge is on.
  3. First strike: the Spearman hits the Bruiser before it swings. Bruiser: 6 → 4.
  4. The Bruiser attacks for 3. Spearman: 2 → 0, removed. Orange will score nothing from h6 next turn.

Order matters. If the Bruiser had gone first, the Spearman would have survived its hit and struck back, and the Bruiser would have taken 4 damage instead of 2.

Questions at the table

FAQ

Can my own units stand on my base?

Yes. An enemy can't step onto an occupied square, so it has to destroy that unit before it can capture. It does block one of your recruiting squares, though, and the unit isn't fighting anywhere else.

Do I have to use all 5 actions?

No, but any you don't use are lost. You can't save actions or move a unit twice.

Can a unit attack without moving?

Yes. Any unit can attack from where it stands. Archers must do this, because they can't move and attack in the same action.

Do walls block arrows?

No. Walls only block movement. Range is counted straight through them.

What if an Archer is attacked by an adjacent enemy?

It can't hit back. Archers need at least 2 squares of space. Keep them behind your Spearmen.

Does the game have a turn limit?

No. Your reserve of 6 reinforcements runs out, and the control points keep scoring, so games end on their own. In testing, most finished in 10 to 15 rounds.

What happens if both control points are held by the same player?

They score 2 points a turn. That's how most points victories happen, so contest at least one.

For playtesters

These are draft rules

They come from thousands of simulated games between computer players. The next things to check at the table:

  • Whether Orange's 2-point head start is right for people. Computer players won 46% as Blue with it, and 58% without it.
  • Whether the free choice of when to recruit gives enough decisions. A reserve draft before the game is a possible addition.
  • Whether parking a unit on your own base makes defending too easy.
