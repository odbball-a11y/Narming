# What the 8-hour dynasty showed

One run (38 championships, 190 races, 30 racers over six generations; six founders with different cars, then foals
bred from retired studs; all-rounder and specialist courses between championships). One seed, noisy ratings:
treat these as leads, not laws.

**Which car won titles:** heavy cars. 19 of the 38 titles and 73 wins from 15 heavy racers, against 7 titles for
medium and 3 for light. Heavy cars were also bred from more, so they were over-represented; per racer, medium
cars had the best mean peak rating (1289 against 1220 heavy and 1216 light).

**Which stars:** the top third by rating had more handling (3.1 v 2.6) and braking (3.1 v 2.8) and less
acceleration (2.4 v 3.1) than the rest. Racers with 3+ handling and braking, whatever their weight, won most.
Temperament mattered little: the top third was slightly less aggressive (0.47 v 0.53).

**Lap records:** held by a light, top-speed-5 car (Fury Thunderbolt: Oval, Speedway, Technical). Fast cars set the
quickest laps; the cars that kept their position and their bodywork in traffic won the titles.

**Specialists:** no advantage over all-rounders in championships (mean peak 1215 v 1245), though specialists did
hold the top single rating (Dash Brick, Esses, 1442). Track kings were mostly all-rounders.

**Later generations:** no clear improvement over six generations (mean peak by generation 1241, 1256, 1193, 1188,
1341, 1275). Foals start from their parents' brains with 3 + 8 generations of schooling, so a foal is about as good
as its parents rather than better. Breeding keeps good traits going; it does not yet compound them.

**Problems found:** the run stalled at 7.1 h because a cap on living racers counted retired studs; fixed (foals
are now bred whenever fewer than six cars are still racing).

Load the final stable into the game with `python -m ai.snapshot load dynasty-final` (the current stable is kept
aside as `stable.before-dynasty-final`), then open the Hall of Fame.
